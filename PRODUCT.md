# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Prospective and current UW–Madison students deciding whether to attend a MadCoders meeting or join the club, plus current members checking meeting logistics (date, time, location) and RSVPing. Secondary audience: club officers managing meetings/submissions through `/admin`, a separate product-register surface.

## Product Purpose

MadCoders (UW-Madison Madison Coders Club) is a student-run organization at UW–Madison for coding, software development, and technical collaboration (TBD for full mission details). The public site's job is to make the club feel credible and worth joining, and to make attending a meeting frictionless — see what's happening, when, where, and RSVP in a couple of clicks. Success looks like a visitor understanding the club's value in seconds and getting involved with no confusion about logistics.

## Brand Personality

Technical-credible and approachable-energetic at once: the confidence of a well-run tech community, worn lightly with student-club warmth. Voice is clear and direct, never stiff or corporate; typography and color should read "considered," not "flashy."

## Anti-references

No specific named anti-references given. General defaults apply: avoid generic SaaS/dashboard aesthetics on public-facing pages, avoid corporate clichés, avoid generic template scaffolding.

## Design Principles

- Credibility first: typography, spacing, and color should read as considered and trustworthy.
- Warmth without informality: approachable tone for developers and beginners alike.
- Logistics are the product: for meetings/events, clarity of date/time/location/RSVP beats decoration every time.
- One brand, two registers: public pages are brand-register (identity, credibility, conversion); `/admin` is product-register (task efficiency).
- UW Cardinal red (`--color-cardinal`, oklch primary) is the identity anchor established in `global.css` — extend it, don't replace it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG 2.1 AA: sufficient color contrast, full keyboard navigability, visible focus states, `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives for any motion.
